What Is the Faith CNAP

The gap between knowing and doing
is the foundation of the Faith CNAP.

It is possible to know what you believe, value Scripture, attend church, pray, and still find that some parts of life are not fully aligned with those beliefs.

The Faith CNAP is a structured Christian assessment designed to help you examine how consistently your faith is being expressed across seven important areas of life: Wellness, Spirituality, Family, Relationships, Career, Finances, and Contribution.

It is not a test of how good a Christian you are. It is a way to see more clearly where your life is well tended, where your beliefs and behaviour are working together, and where greater attention may be needed.

Keep Your Lamps Burning

Jesus used the image of a burning lamp to speak about readiness, attention, and faithful responsibility. The Faith CNAP uses that image as a framework for self-examination. Your life has been entrusted to you — including your body, your relationships, your work, your resources, your influence, and your spiritual life.

The purpose is not perfection. It is awareness, responsibility, and the willingness to ask an important question: Where is my faith clearly visible in the way I live, and where is there still a gap between what I believe and what I practice?

The Foundation

Why Knowing Better
Is Not Always Enough

From Knowing Better to Doing Better

Why do intelligent, sincere people often know what they should do — and still struggle to do it consistently?

My work on the Faith CNAP grew out of the research behind my book, From Knowing Better to Doing Better. The central question was simple: why do intelligent, sincere people often know what they should do and still struggle to do it consistently?

That question led me into neuroscience, behavioural psychology, cultural neuroscience, identity, stress, habit formation, and the role repeated practices play in shaping behaviour. It also led me back to the practical depth of biblical teaching.

Scripture has always dealt with many of the same human challenges: attention, temptation, discipline, forgiveness, responsibility, relationships, stewardship, identity, service, and transformation.

The Faith CNAP brings those ideas together in practical language. It does not suggest that neuroscience proves the Bible, nor does it reduce faith to psychology. Instead, it asks how biblical principles can become visible in everyday behaviour.

The Framework

Seven Areas. One Life.

We often treat different parts of life as though they exist independently — but they do not. Poor health can affect patience. Financial stress strains relationships. Career pressure can damage family life.Unresolved conflict can affect sleep and wellbeing. Spiritual practices can influence how we interpret and respond to all of it.
The Faith CNAP looks at seven areas because a coherent life depends on how those areas interact.

01

Wellness

How you care for your body, energy, recovery, stress, and coping habits.

02

Spirituality

How consistently your relationship with God, convictions, and spiritual practices shape your decisions and behaviour.

03

Family

Presence, communication, responsibility, repair, and the example you are setting.

04

Relationships

Boundaries, honesty, conflict, accountability, and emotional safety in how you engage with others.

05

Career

How you steward your work, abilities, attention, pressure, and direction.

06

Finances

Awareness, planning, restraint, generosity, and integrity in your relationship with money.

07

Contribution

How your experience, abilities, resources, and influence are being used beyond yourself.

Together, these areas create a broader picture of how well your beliefs, choices, and daily life are working together.

The Assessment

How the Assessment Works

The Faith CNAP consists of 35 questions — five within each of the seven domains. Each question is scored using the same developmental progression. The purpose is not to chase the highest score. Each level tells you something different.

1

Disconnected

An area needs attention and intentional focus.

2

Awakening

You see what needs to change but are not yet consistent.

3

Practicing

Meaningful behaviour is becoming established.

4

Integrated

The principle has become a stable part of how you live.

5

Generative

Strength in that area is increasingly benefiting others.

Your results help identify where your faith is already becoming embodied — and where there may be a more important next step.

Important Context

This Is Not Pastoral Counselling

I am not a pastor, and I do not present this work as pastoral counselling or theological authority. My background is in coaching, human behaviour, neuroscience, identity, health, and the practical process of helping people move from insight into action.

The Faith CNAP is designed to complement, not replace, your church, pastor, spiritual community, counselling, or theological study. My role is to help you examine a practical question: If biblical principles matter to me, what should they look like in the way I actually live?

That means looking at stewardship in health, forgiveness during conflict, integrity at work, restraint and generosity with money, responsibility within family, and service beyond self.

“If biblical principles matter to me, what should they look like in the way I actually live?”

Who This Is For

Designed for Christians who want to examine their lives honestly.

The Faith CNAP is not primarily for someone looking for more inspiration. It is for someone looking for greater integration — a more consistent connection between what they believe and how they actually live.

✦

✦

You want to bring greater consistency between belief and behaviour across every area of your life.

The movement is straightforward: first you see clearly, then you begin to practice differently. With enough consistency, practice becomes integrated into character. Over time, what strengthens you can begin to strengthen the people around you.

That is the deeper purpose of the Faith CNAP. The question is not simply, “What do I believe?” The more difficult question is:

“What has what I believe actually produced in the way I live?”

If you are ready to examine that honestly, begin the Faith CNAP.
